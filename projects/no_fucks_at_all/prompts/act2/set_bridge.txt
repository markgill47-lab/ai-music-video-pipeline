A wide shot of a tiny low-budget television studio dressed as the bridge of a 1960s-style starship, built on a hundred-dollar budget out of cardboard and aluminium foil. A curved horseshoe of cardboard command consoles covered in foil and bright construction paper, dotted with bottle-cap buttons, Christmas-tree lights and hand-drawn dials. In the centre a thrift-store swivel armchair spray-painted gold as the captain's chair. Two cardboard helm stations in front of it. The back wall is a big painted viewscreen of black card with glitter stars and a lopsided painted planet. Walls of crinkled foil in orange, red and silver panels, a cardboard doorway on one side. At the edges of the frame the real studio shows: the unpainted plywood backs of the flats, black drape curtains, a lighting grid of fresnel lamps overhead, and cables across the concrete floor.
Photorealistic, 1980s public-access television, shot on 35mm film, fine grain, flat bright studio lighting from overhead fresnels, hugely saturated candy colours.

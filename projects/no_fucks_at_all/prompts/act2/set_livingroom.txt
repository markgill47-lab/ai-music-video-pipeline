A wide shot of a tiny low-budget television studio dressed as a cartoonishly cheap 1980s living room. A lumpy orange-and-brown floral sofa, a shaggy avocado green rug, plywood walls painted with fake wood panelling and a crooked painted picture of a sailboat, a floor lamp with a fringed shade. On a spindly wooden stand at the right stands a big vintage CRT television with an exaggerated bulging bulbous glass screen, a wood-grain cabinet, chunky dials and tall rabbit-ear antennae; on its glowing screen a grey-haired politician in a dark suit and red tie speaks at a podium with a flag behind him. At the edges of the frame the real studio shows: the unpainted plywood backs of the side flats, black drape curtains, a lighting grid of fresnel lamps overhead, and cables across the scuffed grey concrete floor.
Photorealistic, 1980s public-access television, shot on 35mm film, fine grain, flat bright studio lighting from overhead fresnels, hugely saturated candy colours.
